00 / Positioning

From “enterprise AI vendor” to “the engineering team you wish you’d hired.”

The current site sells AI consulting to enterprises in language an international buyer has read a hundred times. This proposal repositions Rockship around what actually differentiates it — senior engineers who take a problem to production — and rebuilds the page so a visitor in San Francisco, Singapore or Berlin knows who we are, what we sell and why to believe us before they scroll.

H1
Senior engineers who ship outcomes, not tickets.
Closest to the sixonefourlabs register. Direct, engineer-to-engineer, sets up the case studies as proof. Reads as confident rather than boastful.
Shown below
H2
The engineering team you’d have built, if you had six months.
Leads with the buyer’s real constraint — hiring time. Strongest for a founder audience; slightly long for a hero.
H3
Product engineers, embedded in your team, shipping in week one.
Most concrete about the model (staff augmentation) and the speed claim. Safest option if we want zero ambiguity about what we sell.
H4
We don’t sell hours. We ship systems that run in production.
Sharpest differentiation against the body-shop category. Risk: contrarian framing can read as defensive on first meeting.
H5
Curated engineering teams out of Ho Chi Minh City. Shipping worldwide.
Leads with geography and “curated.” Works if we’re confident the location is an asset to state up front rather than to answer later.

Three ways to work with us.

Every engagement is staffed with senior engineers who have shipped production systems before. No junior bench, no account-manager layer between you and the people writing the code.

One to five senior engineers who join your standups, your repo and your on-call rotation. They report to your engineering lead and work in your process — we handle contracts, payroll and cover. Month-to-month after the first quarter.

04 / How it works

From first call to first commit.

01

Scoping call

30 minutes on the problem, your stack and how your team works. You leave with a written recommendation on team shape, whether or not you hire us.

02

Engineer shortlist

We put forward named engineers with the relevant production experience. You interview them yourself — we never place someone you haven’t met.

03

Paid trial sprint

Two weeks on real work, in your repo, with defined success criteria. Walk away at the end with no further commitment.

04

Scale or hand over

Grow the pod as the roadmap grows, or run a structured handover to your in-house team. Both are planned from the start.

06 / Open questions

What I need before this can be built.

Turn on “Flag unverified claims” in the top bar to see every place on the page that is currently running on an assumption.

Blocking
Real team numbers. Headcount, average years of experience, and how many engineers are placeable today. The metrics band is the credibility centre of the page and two of its four figures are invented.
Blocking
Team bios with previous companies. This is the single thing sixonefourlabs does that we don’t. Four portraits, four “previously at” lines. Without them the team section is weaker than having no team section.
Decision
Do we name any clients? All five case studies are anonymised. One named logo is worth more than four anonymous ones — worth asking the Singapore clinic group and the food distributor for permission.
Decision
Booking flow. “Book a call” currently goes to a contact form. A real Cal.com or Calendly embed converts materially better for an international audience who won’t wait a day for a reply.
Must fix
The chatbot is claiming things that aren’t true. Its knowledge base states “$50M Series A” and “40+ PhD researchers.” An international prospect asking it a question today gets fabricated credentials. This has to be cleaned up in the same PR.
Must fix
“150+ Enterprise Clients” comes off the page. It isn’t supportable next to five case studies, and it’s the kind of claim a diligent buyer checks. Replaced above with figures we can defend.
Also
Broken social previews. og-image.jpg and twitter-image.jpg are referenced but don’t exist on disk, and metadataBase isn’t set. Both fixed as part of the SEO work in the same PR.
